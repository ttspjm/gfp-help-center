Consistency rule

No single trading day can make up more than 30% of your total profit for the period. On GOAT this applies in the evaluation and the funded stage. On Premium it applies in the funded stage only.

Going over it doesn't close the account. You can't pass or request a payout until your best day is within 30%, and you can keep trading until it is.

In the funded stage it resets with every approved payout and only counts the profit you make in the new payout cycle.

Example: on a $25K GOAT evaluation, the $2,000 profit target means no single day can contribute more than $600. If your best day is $700, you pass once your total profit reaches $2,334.

Maximum drawdown

Your maximum drawdown sets the floor your account must stay above. It's 4%, trailing intraday on GOAT and 5%, static on Premium.

GOAT: how it trails

The floor follows your equity in real time, including open positions. When your equity reaches a new high, the floor moves up and stays 4% of your starting balance below that high. That's $400 on $10K, $1,000 on $25K, $2,000 on $50K and $4,000 on $100K. The floor never moves down.

Example: on a $25K account your floor starts at $24,000. During the day your open positions take your equity to $25,400, so the floor moves up to $24,400. The positions then drop back and you end the day at $25,100. Your floor stays at $24,400.

GOAT: when it locks

Once your equity reaches 4% above your starting balance, the floor reaches your starting balance. It stops trailing and stays there.

In the funded stage, your first payout also locks the floor at your starting balance. From then on, if your equity reaches or falls below your starting balance, the account is breached. See How payouts affect your drawdown.

Premium: a fixed floor

The floor is fixed 5% below your starting balance. It never moves up, so new equity highs and payouts don't change it.

Example: on a $25K account your floor is $23,750 from your first trade onwards.

Starter

Starter uses the same trailing method as GOAT at 4%, or $100. The floor starts at $2,400 and locks at $2,500 once it gets there.

Position and share limits

Open positions

You can hold up to 30 open positions at once across your account.

The limit counts each distinct market and side you hold, and it is the same for every account size. It applies to both evaluation and funded accounts.

Once you have 30 open positions, close one before you open a new one.

This is a ceiling, so you can always trade fewer shares.

What counts as one event

An event means all the Kalshi markets on the same real-world fact, such as the same game, election or data release. Kalshi can split one game into several markets, like the winner, the spread, the total and props. The limit counts all of them together, YES and NO shares combined.

Example: 600 shares on the winner of a game plus 400 on the spread of the same game is 1,000 shares on one event. That's the full limit on a $25K GOAT account.

Entry price range

On GOAT and Premium, your entry price must be between 5¢ and 85¢ when you open a position.

The range only applies when you open a position. A share bought at 60¢ can be held or sold at any price afterwards.

On Starter the maximum entry price is 85¢.

What happens if I fail my evaluation?

Your evaluation fails if you:

  • go below your maximum drawdown floor,
  • lose more than the daily drawdown in one trading day (3% on GOAT, 2.5% on Premium),
  • on GOAT, let your open positions combined lose more than 1% of your current balance, or
  • run out of time before reaching the profit target (120 days).

A failed evaluation can't be restarted and its fee isn't refunded. To try again, buy a new evaluation.

Going over the 30% consistency rule on GOAT doesn't fail the evaluation. You keep trading until your best day is within 30%.

Is there an inactivity rule?

Funded accounts must stay active. Place at least one trade in any rolling 15-day period on GOAT or 30-day period on Premium. If that many calendar days pass without a trade, your funded account can be deactivated.

The rule only applies to funded accounts. There's no inactivity rule during the evaluation, though the 120-day time limit still applies.

Going on a break

If you'll be away, message support before your break starts. As long as you've told us in advance, your funded account won't be deactivated for inactivity while you're away.

Prohibited trading practices

Accounts are for disciplined, risk-managed trading. Any of the practices below can lead to a failed evaluation, a closed funded account and lost payouts.

Market manipulation and information abuse

  • Self-trading or wash trading to move prices or volumes.
  • Trading together with other users to move prices or create fake liquidity.
  • Trading on non-public information, such as early knowledge of how a market resolves.
  • Trying to influence the outcome of an event you have direct control over.

Getting around the limits

  • Building oversized exposure across closely related markets to get around the share limit, for example buying YES on a candidate winning and YES on several state-level markets that add up to the same bet.
  • Abusing known differences between related markets, settlement rules or resolution sources.
  • Buying up most of a thin market to distort its price.

Account abuse

  • Opposite or offsetting positions across several accounts, yours or anyone else's, to hedge or make sure one account passes.
  • Copying trades between accounts, by hand or automatically, including group trading.
  • More than one person trading the same account.
  • Running several accounts from the same device or shared connection.
  • Using a VPN, proxy or any tool that hides or fakes your location.
  • Opening many accounts with coordinated strategies to farm passes.

Risky trading

  • Martingale or doubling up after losses.
  • Putting most of your allowed risk into a single market or event.
  • Gambling-style bets or deliberately breaking a rule to influence the account's outcome.
  • Any technique designed to game the consistency rule or payout rules.

Automated trading

Bots, scripts and any tool that places, manages or changes trades without your manual input are not allowed. We monitor for signs of automation such as inhuman reaction times, highly regular trade timing and identical repeated orders.

Trading alerts and signal services are fine, as long as you place every trade yourself. A service that executes trades for you counts as a bot.

Daily drawdown

Your daily drawdown is 3% on GOAT and 2.5% on Premium. If you lose more than that in one day, the account is breached.

When the day resets

The trading day resets at 5:00 PM ET (New York time). Your limit for the next day is measured from the higher of your balance or equity at that point.

On Starter the daily drawdown is 2%, which is $50 at the $2,500 starting balance.

Maximum floating loss

On GOAT, your open positions combined can't lose more than 1% of your current balance. The limit applies in the evaluation and in the funded stage. Going over it closes the account.

Premium and Starter have no floating loss limit.

It counts all your open positions together

The limit looks at the combined open loss across every position you hold, not each position on its own.

Example: on a $25K account with a $25,000 balance, the limit is $250. One open position is down $150 and another is down $120. Neither is over $250 alone, but together they're down $270, so the limit is broken.

Profit above the cap

Profit above the cap stays in your funded account as equity. It does not carry forward as withdrawable profit into the next payout cycle.

Payout cycles

Every approved payout starts a new payout cycle. Your withdrawable profit resets to $0, and your equity after the payout becomes your new baseline. If your equity falls below your baseline, new profit first rebuilds it.
